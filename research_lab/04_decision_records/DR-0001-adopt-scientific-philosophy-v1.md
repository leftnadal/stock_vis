## 1. Context

Stock_vis ultimately exists to support better investment decisions. For the Research Lab to contribute to that purpose, however, producing higher-performing models or larger volumes of analysis is not sufficient by itself.

Early discussions treated the Research Lab primarily as an organization for building better models. As the discussion developed, a more fundamental relationship became clear: models are not the purpose of research, but tools for understanding reality.

The Research Lab therefore required a direct research purpose distinct from, but supportive of, Stock_vis’s ultimate purpose:

> **To develop a better understanding of reality.**

Without a shared philosophy, the Research Lab would face several structural risks:

- model performance or complexity could take precedence over understanding reality;
- unexplained aspects of reality could be dismissed as noise or exceptions in order to preserve an existing model;
- questions, available data, or preferred methods could be selected before a genuine problem in reality had been identified;
- evidence could be used to defend an existing conclusion rather than to change current understanding;
- models could absorb every failure through after-the-fact exceptions and become immune to challenge; and
- human researchers and non-human research systems could interpret and handle reality, models, and evidence according to inconsistent standards.

The Research Lab therefore needed a common, highest-level research philosophy that would not depend on a particular technology, model, investment strategy, or current implementation.

The Scientific Philosophy was established to answer the following question:

> **What kind of researcher must one be in order to develop a better understanding of reality?**

Here, “researcher” is a role term that includes both human researchers and non-human research systems engaged in research.

## 2. Decision

The Stock_vis Research Lab adopts the following purpose, five principles, philosophical system, and scope of application as the **Scientific Philosophy v1**.

### 2.1 Direct Purpose of the Research Lab

The direct purpose of the Stock_vis Research Lab is:

> **To develop a better understanding of reality.**

This purpose does not replace Stock_vis’s ultimate purpose of supporting better investment decisions.

```text
Stock_vis ultimate purpose
→ Better Investment Decisions

Research Lab direct purpose
→ Better Understanding of Reality
```

The Research Lab supports better judgment and investment decisions by developing a better understanding of reality. Better understanding does not automatically guarantee better decisions, but it provides the research foundation on which better judgment can be built.

### 2.2 The Five Principles

The Scientific Philosophy v1 adopts the following five principles:

1. **Reality takes precedence over every model.**
2. **Models exist as tools for understanding reality.**
3. **Research begins with unexplained reality.**
4. **Better evidence advances understanding.**
5. **Every model must be falsifiable.**

Their order is logical rather than procedural:

```text
What is the final standard?
→ Reality

Why do models exist?
→ Model

Where does research originate?
→ Research

What advances understanding?
→ Evidence

What condition must every model satisfy?
→ Falsifiability
```

### 2.3 Philosophical System

The five principles do not form a collection of independent declarations. Together, they constitute a coherent philosophical system for developing understanding of reality.

Within this system:

```text
Reality
→ the continuing object of observation and the final standard

Understanding
→ the central purpose of the Research Lab

Model
→ a core tool for understanding reality
  and expressing current understanding

Unexplained reality
→ an explanatory or unresolved gap between reality
  and current understanding

A research undertaking
→ an investigation originating in that gap
  and directed toward advancing understanding

Evidence
→ grounds that advance understanding
  through critical examination and interpretation

Falsifiability
→ the structural condition that keeps models
  open to challenge from reality and evidence
```

Understanding is not one stage within a linear research cycle. It is the central purpose that the entire system continually seeks to advance.

Falsifiability is not the final stage of research. It is the condition that prevents models from becoming closed structures capable of preserving themselves under every possible outcome.

In the third principle, “research begins” refers to the origin and justification of a research undertaking. It does not refer to the formal Research stage defined within the Research Protocol.

### 2.4 Boundary Between Philosophy and Lower-Level Documents

The following division of responsibilities is adopted:

```text
Founding Principles
→ define Stock_vis’s ultimate purpose and highest-level commitments

Scientific Philosophy
→ defines the highest-level attitudes and principles
  of the Research Lab

Research Methodology
→ translates those principles into repeatable
  research processes and judgment rules

Evaluation Methodology
→ defines how research, evidence, and models
  are judged in accordance with the philosophy

Model Documents
→ record how the philosophy is applied,
  tested, limited, and revised in individual models
```

Lower-level documents must remain consistent with the Scientific Philosophy. They must not silently ignore or redefine its principles.

Research and evaluation may reveal limitations in the Scientific Philosophy itself. When such a conflict is found, it must be made explicit, its consequences analyzed, and the relevant document reconsidered through discussion and approved revision.

### 2.5 Applicability

The Scientific Philosophy applies to both human researchers and non-human research systems.

It defines the principles and Research Implications shared by all research actors. It does not prescribe the concrete behavior of a particular system.

Concrete procedures, behavior rules, judgment sequences, and evaluation criteria are defined in the Research Methodology and Evaluation Methodology.

## 3. Alternatives Considered

The following alternatives were considered during the formation of the Scientific Philosophy v1.

Not selecting an alternative does not mean that the underlying activity is unimportant. Several alternatives remain valuable within Methodology, Evaluation, Model development, product design, or individual research programs. They were not selected as the Research Lab’s highest-level purpose or organizing principle.

### 3.1 Better Models as the Direct Purpose

Model development is an important research activity, and performance improvements are often measurable and operationally useful.

This alternative was not selected as the highest-level purpose because better evidence may justify restricting, replacing, or discarding an existing model. Such outcomes can represent successful advances in understanding rather than failures to produce a better model.

```text
Research Lab purpose
→ Better Understanding of Reality

Model
→ A core tool for that purpose
```

### 3.2 Better Prediction or Better Investment Decisions as the Direct Research Purpose

Prediction and decision support are central to the practical value of Stock_vis.

They were not selected as the Research Lab’s direct purpose because predictive performance can be mistaken for sufficient understanding, and because equating the Research Lab’s purpose with Stock_vis’s ultimate product purpose would blur the distinction between research and decision support.

```text
Better Understanding of Reality
→ supports Better Judgment
→ supports Better Investment Decisions
```

### 3.3 Understanding as a Sixth Principle

A separate principle such as “Research seeks better understanding” was considered.

It was not adopted because understanding is not one requirement alongside the five principles. It is the purpose toward which all five principles are directed.

```text
Purpose
→ Better Understanding of Reality

Five Principles
→ the philosophical conditions through which
  understanding remains grounded in reality
```

### 3.4 Understanding as One Stage in a Linear Cycle

The following linear structure was considered:

```text
Reality
→ Model
→ Understanding
→ Unexplained Reality
→ Research
→ Evidence
→ Better Understanding
```

This structure was rejected because it made understanding appear to be an intermediate output and made the philosophical system resemble a competing Research Protocol.

It also suggested that unexplained reality emerges only after understanding, rather than being an aspect of reality that current understanding has failed to explain or resolve.

Understanding was therefore placed at the center of the system rather than inside a linear sequence.

### 3.5 Research Beginning with a Research Question

Research Questions remain essential, but they can be generated around available data, a preferred method, or a model that researchers wish to improve without first identifying a genuine unresolved condition in reality.

The following prior relationship was therefore adopted:

```text
Reality
→ Research Trigger
→ Research Problem
→ Research Question
```

The concrete definitions and procedures governing this relationship belong to the Research Methodology.

### 3.6 Better Evidence Producing Better Models

The following principle was considered:

> Better evidence produces better models.

It was not selected because it limits the role of evidence to model improvement.

Better evidence may strengthen a model, but it may also restrict its scope, reveal inadequate assumptions, require a different model, or produce a fundamentally different understanding.

The broader principle was therefore adopted:

> **Better evidence advances understanding.**

### 3.7 Falsifiability as Only a Final Evaluation Step

Falsifiability could have been treated solely as a criterion applied during final model evaluation.

That position was insufficient because it would make falsifiability appear to be a late-stage test rather than a condition that must keep models open to challenge throughout research.

The Scientific Philosophy therefore treats falsifiability as a structural condition of every model. Its concrete specification and testing remain responsibilities of the Evaluation Methodology and Model Documents.

### 3.8 Concrete Research-System Behavior in the Scientific Philosophy

Concrete rules such as evidence-review order, trigger-selection criteria, model-revision procedures, reporting rules, and approval points could have been included directly within the Scientific Philosophy.

This alternative was not selected because it would make the highest-level philosophy dependent on current systems and procedures.

```text
Scientific Philosophy
→ shared principles and Research Implications

Research Methodology
→ concrete procedures, behaviors, and judgment rules
```

### 3.9 Specific Research Topics as Philosophical Principles

Specific topics such as black swans, market momentum, supply chains, or particular investment risks could have been included as philosophical principles.

They were not included because they are research topics or research programs conducted under the philosophy, not parts of the philosophy itself.

The Scientific Philosophy provides the basis for investigating such topics by requiring models and current understanding to remain open to unexplained reality and contrary evidence.

## 4. Rationale

The Scientific Philosophy v1 places a better understanding of reality at the center of the Research Lab so that no particular model, method, or technical achievement can become the final purpose of research.

The five principles are mutually dependent.

Reality must take precedence so that unexplained observations are not excluded merely to preserve a model. Models must remain tools so that their revision or rejection can count as legitimate research progress. Research must originate in unexplained reality so that questions and methods remain connected to genuine gaps in current understanding. Evidence must be capable of changing understanding so that research does not become the defense of predetermined conclusions. Models must be falsifiable so that reality and evidence can meaningfully challenge them.

No single principle is sufficient by itself:

```text
Reality cannot truly take precedence
if models are immune to challenge.

Models cannot remain tools for understanding
if research is organized around preserving them.

Research cannot advance understanding
if evidence is not allowed to affect conclusions.

Evidence cannot meaningfully affect understanding
if models can accommodate every possible result.
```

Separating Philosophy from Methodology preserves both stability and adaptability.

The Scientific Philosophy remains stable across changes in technology, systems, and procedures. The Research Methodology can continue to develop concrete practices in response to new reality and research experience, while remaining accountable to the same higher-level principles.

Applying the philosophy to both human and non-human research systems establishes a common standard without requiring identical implementations.

The decision therefore adopts not merely five desirable statements, but an integrated research philosophy in which:

> **Reality is the final standard, understanding is the central purpose, models are tools, research originates in unexplained reality, evidence advances understanding, and no model is exempt from meaningful challenge.**

## 5. Consequences

### 5.1 Binding Implications

The adoption of the Scientific Philosophy v1 establishes the following requirements:

- Every formal research undertaking must be traceable to an unresolved condition identified in reality.
- The Research Methodology must translate the five principles into repeatable processes and judgment rules.
- The Evaluation Methodology must assess more than performance alone, including evidence, assumptions, scope, limitations, and falsifiability where relevant.
- Model Documents must record models as challengeable research outputs rather than only as implementations or performance results.
- Evidence must be capable of substantively affecting current understanding.
- Human researchers and non-human research systems must share the same highest-level philosophy.
- Conflicts between the philosophy and lower-level documents must be made explicit and resolved through approved revision.
- Material changes to the purpose, principles, philosophical system, applicability, or Philosophy–Methodology boundary require a new approved version and an appropriate Decision Record.

These requirements do not predetermine the concrete procedures, metrics, templates, or thresholds by which they will be implemented.

### 5.2 Expected Benefits

The decision is expected to provide:

- a common highest-level standard across research actors;
- a clear distinction between model performance and research purpose;
- a basis for treating unexplained reality as a research resource;
- stronger protection against confirmation-driven use of evidence;
- a structural safeguard against self-protecting models;
- clearer justification for Methodology, Evaluation, and Model-documentation requirements; and
- a research philosophy stable across changes in technology and implementation.

### 5.3 Costs and Limitations

The decision also creates costs and unresolved implementation challenges:

- additional documentation and review will be required;
- not every instance of unexplained reality can become a research project, making prioritization necessary;
- specifying falsifiability may be difficult for complex or early-stage models;
- conflicting evidence will require explicit methods for comparison and interpretation;
- shared terminology will be needed to prevent concept drift across documents;
- the philosophy alone does not guarantee correct data, valid interpretation, successful models, complete understanding, or better investment decisions; and
- the philosophy must remain stable enough to guide lower-level documents while remaining open to revision when reality reveals its limitations.

These matters must be addressed in lower-level documents and future research.

## 6. Scope and Non-Decisions

### 6.1 In Scope

DR-0001 decides:

- the direct purpose of the Research Lab;
- the five principles and their logical order;
- the central position of understanding within the philosophical system;
- the relationship among reality, models, research, evidence, and falsifiability;
- the boundary between Scientific Philosophy and lower-level documents; and
- the application of the philosophy to human researchers and non-human research systems.

### 6.2 Out of Scope

DR-0001 does not decide:

- the concrete stages, classifications, templates, or approval rules of the Research Protocol;
- evidence-scoring methods, evaluation metrics, benchmarks, statistical tests, or thresholds;
- the behavior rules of a particular research system;
- the final template or validation method for individual Model Documents;
- the adoption or prioritization of individual models, investment strategies, or research topics;
- specific technologies, algorithms, software architecture, product features, or data systems;
- documentation language, file organization, metadata, versioning, Change Log, or terminology-governance rules; or
- changes to existing code, product behavior, data, or model implementations.

DR-0001 establishes the philosophical requirements that lower-level documents must address. It does not predetermine their solutions.
