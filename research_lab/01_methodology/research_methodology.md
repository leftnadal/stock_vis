## 1. Purpose

The purpose of the Research Methodology is to define how the Stock_vis Research Lab turns unresolved reality into disciplined research activity and how research moves through its lifecycle without confusing workflow, evaluation, and epistemic admission.

The Research Methodology operationalizes the Scientific Philosophy and the Knowledge and Understanding Framework. It does not redefine Reality, Understanding, Research Knowledge, or the epistemic architecture established by those higher-level authorities.

The Methodology is designed to support the Research Lab's direct purpose of developing a better understanding of reality while preventing several recurrent failures:

- beginning research from an available question, dataset, method, or model without a legitimate unresolved condition in reality;
- treating Purpose or Research Primitives as sequential workflow stages;
- forcing every research undertaking into a mandatory hypothesis-testing pattern;
- requiring Engineering when no artifact needs to be built;
- treating Evaluation as a one-time terminal gate rather than a cross-cutting challenge function;
- treating Knowledge as the mandatory outcome of every research undertaking;
- allowing evaluation results to silently become epistemic admission decisions; and
- allowing exploratory discovery to be presented as independent confirmation without additional warrant.

## 2. Scope and Authority

The Research Methodology domain holds normative authority over the Stock_vis Research Lab's general research lifecycle and operational research process. This document is the canonical Authority Source and resolution point through which that authority is expressed for v1.

It governs:

- Research Triggers, Problems, Questions, and Research Design;
- the role of Hypotheses within research;
- exploratory and confirmatory research postures;
- Investigation and the activities that may occur within it;
- the interface between Research Methodology and Evaluation Methodology;
- lifecycle actions following evaluation;
- the process by which candidate results may be admitted as Research Knowledge;
- the high-level separation between epistemic admission authority and consequential governance;
- the handling of unresolved, revised, restricted, or discontinued research;
- the relationship between Knowledge change, Understanding impact, and subsequent Research Triggers; and
- proportionality in research process rigor.

This document does not define:

- the philosophical meaning of Reality, Understanding, Models, Evidence, or Research;
- the substantive definition of Research Knowledge or the Knowledge–Understanding relationship;
- evaluation dimensions, evidence weights, validation criteria, or admission thresholds;
- model-specific algorithms, metrics, or implementation;
- ontology, database, graph, or software schemas;
- exact agent voting, orchestration, reviewer-count, or escalation algorithms; or
- investment decision rules.

Those responsibilities remain with their legitimate authorities.

## 3. Governing Context

### 3.1 Purpose Is a Constraint, Not a Workflow Stage

The Research Lab's direct purpose is to develop a better understanding of reality. Purpose constrains and justifies research activity across the lifecycle; it is not a step that a research undertaking passes through once.

### 3.2 Research Primitives Are Semantic Lenses, Not Workflow Stages

Research Primitives structure how the Research Lab describes relevant aspects of reality and research objects. They are not sequential actions in the Research Protocol.

For Research Methodology v1, the current operational primitive set is:

- Market
- Entity
- Relationship
- State
- Transition
- Decision

This set is a methodological structuring lens. Its use here does not claim universal semantic authority over every possible meaning of these concepts, and later approved work may reconsider the set if research requires it.

### 3.3 Upstream Constraints

The methodology must remain consistent with these upstream commitments:

- Reality takes precedence over every model.
- Models exist as tools for understanding reality.
- Research begins when reality reveals a limit in current understanding.
- Better evidence must remain capable of changing current understanding.
- Models must remain open to meaningful challenge.
- Evidence, Claims, Hypothesis roles, Evaluation activities, Knowledge admission, and Understanding must remain epistemically distinct.

## 4. Core Research Lifecycle

The core lifecycle is:

```text
Reality
  ↓
Research Trigger
  ↓
Research Problem
  ↓
Research Question
  ↓
Research Design / Strategy
  ↓
Investigation
  ↕
Cross-cutting Evaluation / Challenge
  ↓
Candidate Research Output(s)
  ↓
Formal Evaluation
  ├─ required for Knowledge admission
  └─ otherwise applied when materially required
  ↓
Structured Evaluation Result
  ↓
Lifecycle / Admission Action
  ├─ continue or extend research
  ├─ revise or reframe
  ├─ restrict scope or conditions
  ├─ remain unresolved
  ├─ stop or discontinue
  └─ admit a bounded result as Research Knowledge
          ↓
Epistemic Impact Check
  ├─ Knowledge lineage implications
  ├─ Understanding reassessment when material
  ├─ Model implications when material
  └─ possible new Research Trigger
```

This is a lifecycle architecture rather than a rigid linear pipeline. Research may return to earlier activities when new evidence, evaluation, or changes in reality justify doing so.

Formal evaluation is not required at the same burden for every research activity. However, admission of a candidate as Research Knowledge always requires an admission-purpose structured evaluation under the applicable Evaluation Methodology. The rigor of that evaluation remains proportional to epistemic consequence.

## 5. Research Trigger

A Research Trigger is a recognized signal that current understanding, models, or methods may be insufficient for an aspect of reality that matters to the Research Lab's purpose.

The current general trigger classes are:

- Observation
- Failure
- Opportunity
- External Change
- Knowledge Gap
- User Need

A trigger does not automatically become a research undertaking. Its significance, relevance, feasibility, expected epistemic value, and materiality may be considered before formal research begins.

## 6. Research Problem

A Research Problem frames what current understanding cannot adequately explain, resolve, characterize, or support.

The Problem must remain traceable to the Trigger that justified the undertaking. It should describe the unresolved condition rather than prematurely encode a preferred answer.

A broad Trigger may reveal more than one Research Problem, and a Problem may later be split, narrowed, or reframed as research develops.

## 7. Research Question

A Research Question specifies what must be investigated in order to advance the Research Problem.

The Question is not the origin of research justification. Its legitimacy derives from the Problem and ultimately from the unresolved condition in reality that generated the Trigger.

Questions may be descriptive, comparative, explanatory, predictive, evaluative, design-oriented, or otherwise appropriate to the Problem. Research Methodology v1 does not adopt an exhaustive question taxonomy.

## 8. Research Design and Strategy

Research Design is the explicit methodological function that connects a Question to Investigation.

A Research Design should be proportional to consequence and uncertainty. It may define, where material:

- the research scope and boundaries;
- the intended research posture;
- the evidence strategy;
- material comparison groups or alternatives;
- methods, models, or analytical approaches;
- material assumptions;
- known threats to inference;
- what evidence would challenge the preferred interpretation;
- the intended evaluation purpose; and
- any pre-specified tests or criteria required for confirmatory work.

Low-consequence and easily reversible research may use a lightweight design. High-consequence, highly reusable, or confirmatory research may require stronger pre-specification and challenge.

Research Design is not intended to create documentation bureaucracy. Its purpose is to make material methodological choices visible before they become hidden assumptions in results.

## 9. Exploratory and Confirmatory Postures

### 9.1 Exploratory Research

Exploratory research seeks patterns, candidate relationships, hypotheses, variables, explanations, or new questions when current understanding does not yet justify a narrow pre-specified test.

Exploratory work may generate Research Claims or Hypotheses during Investigation.

### 9.2 Confirmatory Research

Confirmatory research tests a sufficiently specified Claim or Hypothesis against evidence under a pre-declared design appropriate to the intended inference.

### 9.3 Exploration–Confirmation Separation

A Claim discovered through exploratory analysis must not be treated as independently confirmed merely because the same evidence used to generate the Claim is also compatible with it.

Where stronger confirmation is materially necessary, additional warrant may require independent data, out-of-sample testing, distinct evidence streams, pre-specified analysis, replication, or another method appropriate to the research question.

Exploratory and confirmatory are research postures, not an exhaustive taxonomy of all research types.

## 10. Hypothesis as a Research Role

A Hypothesis is not a mandatory stage for every research undertaking.

A Research Claim takes a Hypothesis role when it is made explicitly investigable and open to evidential challenge. Some Questions begin with an explicit Hypothesis; other exploratory Questions may generate one only after Investigation has begun.

The historical Hypothesis role remains traceable even when the associated Claim is later admitted as Research Knowledge, revised, restricted, or rejected.

## 11. Investigation

Investigation is the methodological space in which the Research Design is executed and evidence is generated, acquired, interpreted, compared, or synthesized.

Activities may include, when appropriate:

- evidence acquisition and source examination;
- measurement and data preparation;
- descriptive or statistical analysis;
- comparison;
- causal or mechanistic analysis;
- modeling;
- engineering and artifact construction;
- experiment or simulation;
- literature or document synthesis;
- qualitative interpretation;
- adversarial or alternative-explanation analysis; and
- synthesis of candidate research outputs.

No activity is mandatory merely because it appears in this list.

### 11.1 Engineering Is Conditional

Engineering is used when research requires construction, implementation, instrumentation, automation, simulation, or another artifact. It is not a universal research stage.

Material engineering work that affects epistemic results should remain traceable through appropriate provenance even when Engineering is not represented as a separate lifecycle state.

### 11.2 Models Are Tools, Not Workflow Stages

Models may be used during Investigation to observe, analyze, generate, test, express, support, or challenge epistemic objects. A Model or Model Output does not become Research Knowledge by origin.

## 12. Evaluation Interface

Evaluation Methodology and Research Methodology are distinct normative systems.

Research Methodology asks:

> What are we investigating, how are we proceeding, and what happens next?

Evaluation Methodology asks:

> How well is this object warranted or fit for its declared purpose and scope, where is it weak, and what remains uncertain or unassessed?

Evaluation may occur throughout the lifecycle as proportional challenge and may also occur as a formal checkpoint before material transitions. Every Knowledge admission requires an admission-purpose structured evaluation, although the evaluation rigor may be lightweight or strong according to consequence and context.

Research Methodology does not redefine evaluation criteria. It submits evaluable objects and contexts to Evaluation Methodology and receives structured assessments.

## 13. Candidate Research Outputs

Investigation may produce many legitimate outputs, including:

- new or revised Research Claims;
- supported or challenged Hypotheses;
- Evidence items, evidence collections, or Claim-relative Evidence Body views;
- models or model outputs;
- bounded candidate epistemic results;
- unresolved alternatives;
- identified Knowledge Gaps;
- negative or null findings;
- revised Research Problems or Questions; and
- candidate Research Knowledge.

Research Knowledge is therefore one possible research outcome, not the mandatory terminal state of every undertaking.

### 13.1 Authority Review for Material Meta-Level Change Candidates

Research may produce material findings or proposals about the Research Lab's own methodology, architecture, governed concepts, authority boundaries, semantic responsibilities, or other cross-case normative structure. Such a finding may arise legitimately during Investigation, but it must not become a formal change candidate merely because it appears useful in one Research Case.

Before a material meta-level finding is advanced as a formal change candidate, the Research Lab must proportionally review it against the relevant currently effective Authority Sources when either:

- the finding would establish meaning, rules, responsibilities, or structure intended to apply beyond the current Research Case; or
- the finding would move, narrow, expand, replace, or materially reinterpret a responsibility or boundary already assigned by an existing authority, methodology, architecture, or intentional Non-Decision.

The review should determine, to the extent material:

- the applicable semantic or methodological jurisdiction and currently effective Authority Sources;
- whether existing authority already covers the finding;
- whether the finding is consistent with upstream constraints and existing authority boundaries;
- whether the current architecture can accommodate the need without creating a new normative structure; and
- whether an apparent conflict reflects an incorrect interpretation or proposal, or material Reality or Evidence that justifies possible Reconsideration of current authority.

Existing authority is not immune from challenge. When Reality or better Evidence materially challenges a current methodology, architecture, decision, or assumption, the finding should be routed through the applicable Reconsideration process rather than silently rejected or silently implemented.

This review is proportional and does not require every local working label, reversible implementation experiment, analytical choice, or case-specific research judgment to undergo formal authority review merely because it may later prove reusable.

Exact retrieval procedures, classification labels, agent orchestration, reviewer counts, automation, and escalation mechanics remain implementation-level or operational Non-Decisions unless separately approved.

## 14. Lifecycle, Admission, and Governance Actions

A structured evaluation result does not automatically determine a lifecycle action.

The Research Methodology interprets evaluation results within the research lifecycle and may take actions such as:

- continue or extend Investigation;
- acquire new evidence;
- revise a Claim;
- reframe the Problem or Question;
- restrict scope, conditions, or intended use;
- preserve an unresolved state;
- discontinue or defer the research path;
- escalate a material unresolved issue under applicable governance; or
- admit a bounded result as Research Knowledge when applicable admission criteria are satisfied.

Exact lifecycle labels remain open to later operational refinement.

### 14.1 Admission Is Not Truth Declaration

Admission means that the Research Lab currently accepts a bounded epistemic result for legitimate reuse under stated scope, conditions, provenance, and limitations. It does not establish timeless or infallible truth.

Evaluation Methodology owns epistemic criteria for admission. Research Methodology owns the process and lifecycle transition by which an evaluated candidate may be admitted.

### 14.2 Epistemic Admission Authority

The Research Lab's epistemic system is responsible for determining whether a candidate result is sufficiently warranted to be admitted as Research Knowledge or maintained as an evaluated Understanding.

The Project Owner / CEO is not the default epistemic truth reviewer and does not serve as the ordinary authority for deciding whether a factual, causal, predictive, comparative, or other research conclusion is scientifically or analytically correct.

Greater epistemic consequence, expected reuse, uncertainty, or disagreement should increase the rigor of Evaluation, effective challenge, and specialized epistemic review. It does not by itself transfer truth adjudication to the Project Owner / CEO.

Epistemic admission therefore remains governed by the approved Research and Evaluation Methodologies. Exact agent-level mechanics, reviewer counts, voting rules, consensus algorithms, and escalation thresholds remain Non-Decisions of v1.2.

### 14.3 Consequential Governance Authority

The Project Owner / CEO holds consequential governance authority over what Stock_vis should do about sufficiently evaluated research results when those results create material strategic, architectural, operational, or value-dependent consequences.

CEO-level governance may include decisions about:

- major research direction or priority;
- substantial resource commitment;
- system-wide or cross-lab dependency;
- methodology or architecture change;
- material downstream use boundaries or risk tolerance;
- product or decision-support direction;
- hard-to-reverse commitments; and
- value choices among multiple legitimate alternatives.

The importance of an epistemic result may justify stronger internal evaluation without requiring CEO epistemic approval. CEO escalation is driven by consequential governance needs rather than by a requirement for the CEO to verify research truth.

The Project Owner / CEO may raise a material epistemic concern and require Re-evaluation or Reconsideration through the applicable methodology. Such a governance request creates a review obligation but does not predetermine the epistemic outcome. The Project Owner / CEO does not directly overwrite Research Knowledge or Understanding by decree; any epistemic revision, restriction, retention, or rejection must proceed through the legitimate Research and Evaluation authorities.

## 15. Knowledge and Understanding Impact

Admission or revision of Knowledge does not automatically create or revise an Understanding.

Material Knowledge changes should prompt an epistemic impact check that asks whether:

- no material Understanding is affected;
- an existing Understanding's support or structure should be re-evaluated;
- scope or boundary conditions should change;
- competing Understandings are affected differently;
- a new synthesis is warranted; or
- a new Research Trigger has emerged.

A Knowledge change should propagate review obligations through material dependencies, not automatically propagate downstream conclusions.

## 16. Research Proportionality

Research rigor and documentation should be proportional to factors such as:

- epistemic consequence;
- downstream dependency and expected reuse;
- uncertainty;
- novelty and complexity;
- difficulty of reversal;
- material conflict in evidence; and
- consequences of being wrong.

Proportionality must not be used to redefine epistemic quality. Urgent or exploratory work may legitimately proceed with incomplete evidence when limitations and intended use remain explicit.

## 17. Re-evaluation and Reconsideration

Evaluation Methodology is the canonical methodological Authority Source for the definitions of **Re-evaluation Trigger** and **Reconsideration Trigger** in v1.

Research Methodology uses those triggers to reopen relevant research lifecycle work when required. A Re-evaluation Trigger may require renewed assessment of a research object or prior evaluation, while a Reconsideration Trigger may reopen a methodology, architecture, decision, or assumption to consider whether it should be retained, modified, restricted, or replaced.

Neither trigger determines the conclusion in advance. A trigger creates a review obligation, not an automatic instruction to change.

Concrete trigger thresholds are delegated to later operational and meta-evaluation work.

## 18. Traceability and Historical Reproducibility

Material research choices and epistemic transitions should remain sufficiently traceable to reproduce why a research path produced a material output and how that output entered later research.

Traceability must be proportional. The Research Lab does not require preservation of every intermediate thought, discarded idea, or agent message.

The exact storage representation is delegated to later ontology and engineering work.

## 19. Boundaries and Non-Decisions

Research Methodology v1.2 does not adopt:

- a fixed exhaustive taxonomy of research question types;
- a fixed exhaustive taxonomy of research methods;
- a mandatory Hypothesis for every research undertaking;
- a mandatory Engineering stage;
- one universal formal Research Design template;
- exact Knowledge admission thresholds;
- exact lifecycle vocabulary;
- exact agent-level admission mechanics, reviewer counts, voting rules, consensus algorithms, or escalation thresholds;
- exact authority-retrieval procedures, meta-finding classification labels, or authority-review automation;
- numerical credibility scales;
- fixed evaluation-rigor tiers;
- a database, ontology, graph, or workflow-engine schema;
- a physical Material Warrant Network implementation; or
- investment decision rules.

These matters remain subject to future approved work and may be reconsidered when evidence from actual Research Lab operation justifies greater specificity.
